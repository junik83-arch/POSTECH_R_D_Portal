> 김용태 교수는 포스텍(POSTECH) 신소재공학 분야 교수로, 전극물성연구실(LEMP, Laboratory for Electrode Material Property)을 이끌고 있습니다. 서강대학교 화학공학과에서 학사(2000)와 석사(2002) 학위를 받은 뒤 일본 JAIST 재료과학 전공에서 박사학위(2006)를 취득했으며, 부산대 산업대학원 부원장(2010~2012)과 미국 Argonne National Laboratory 방문교수(2012~2013)를 거쳤습니다. 학회·학술 활동으로는 Journal of Mechanical Science and Technology 부편집장 및 Business Director, 한국전기화학회와 한국XAFS학회 총무이사, ECS PEFC 심포지엄 한국 대표 오거나이저 등을 맡고 있습니다. 연구 분야는 연료전지, 이차전지, 수전해, CO2 환원, 내식성 소재, 배터리 리사이클링 등 전극 소재와 전기화학 전반이며, 홈페이지에는 183편의 논문과 43건의 등록 특허, 95건의 출원 특허가 등재되어 있습니다. 또한 과학기술정보통신부의 대규모 수소 국제공동연구(H2GATHER) 사업에 최종 선정된 포스텍 컨소시엄에 참여하고 있으며, 학부 '에너지소재'와 대학원 '신소재공학특론: 소재 전기화학' 과목을 강의하고 있습니다.

## 연구관심분야
- Electrocatalysis for energy conversion like fuel cell, electrolysis cell, metal-air battery, and CO2 conversion
- Cathode material of lithium ion batteries and their recycling
- Electrochemical corrosion and anti-corrosion for ferrous and non-ferrous metals
- Synchrotron analysis on electronic and fine structure of electrocatalysts

Fuel Cell
A fuel cell uses the chemical energy of hydrogen or another fuel to cleanly and efficiently produce electricity. If hydrogen is the fuel, electricity, water, and heat are the only products. Fuel cells are unique in terms of the variety of their potential applications; they can provide power for systems as large as a utility power station and as small as a laptop computer. Fuel cells can operate at higher efficiencies than combustion engines, and can convert the chemical energy in the fuel to electrical energy with efficiencies of up to 60%. Fuel cells have lower emissions than combustion engines. Hydrogen fuel cells emit only water, so there are no carbon dioxide emissions and no air pollutants that create smog and cause health problems at the point of operation. Also, fuel cells are quiet during operation as they have fewer moving parts.
Battery
Recent development of Electrical vehicles, and portable devices requires high capacity and energy density batteries. Many other researches were conducted to develop electrical performance of the batteries. Such as doping, electrode surface modification and etc. However closer research in atomic and electronic scale research was barely accomplished. Our group closely investigate the structural or electrical phenomenon, utilizing PLS(Pohang Light Source). We expect this research will provide fundamental understanding of battery performance in atomic scale and further development of high capacity and energy density energy.
Water Electrolysis
Water electrolysis(also called water splitting) is the decomposition of water into oxygen and hydrogen gas due to the passage of an electric current. This technique can be used to make hydrogen gas, a main component of hydrogen fuel, and breathable oxygen gas. It ideally requires a potential difference of 1.23 V to split water. However, real water electrolyzers require higher voltages for the reaction to proceed. The part that exceeds 1.23 V is called overpotential, and represents any kind of loss and nonideality in the electrochemical process. Electrocatalysts can facilitate this reaction.
CO2 Reduction
Carbon dioxide is an abundant potential carbon resource on Earth, and it is also a greenhouse gas whose atmospheric concentration has increased sharply over the past two centuries Chemical fixation of carbon dioxide (CO2) is an attractive technique for utilization of carbon resources, as well as for the reduction of the atmospheric concentration of CO2. Carbon dioxide is a potential carbon resource abundant on earth. The electrochemical reduction of CO2 is the conversion of carbon dioxide to more reduced chemical species. The first examples of electrochemical reduction of carbon dioxide are from the 19th century, when carbon dioxide was reduced to carbon monoxide using a zinc cathode. Research in this field intensified in the 1980s following the oil embargoes of the 1970s. CO2 can be removed by the reduction that produces various chemicals, such as carbon monoxide (CO), methanol (CH3OH), methane (CH4), and formic acid (HCOOH). In our lab, we are mainly focused on the CO2 reduction to CO using various metallic catalysts.
Anti-corrosion
In modern times, with the development of stainless steel, the corrosion resistance of steel has improved dramatically. However, corrosion in extreme conditions such as cryogenic, high temperatures, abrasion and seawater environments still causes economical loss and environmental damage and has become a major cause of safety issues. Since it is almost impossible to replace and repair corrosion parts in such a harsh environment, the need for structural materials with strong corrosion resistance is increasing day by day. Our group is exploring ways to improve the anti-corrosion properties of structural materials without losing mechanical properties via alloy composition control or surface treatment.
Battery Recycling
In order to meet global target of carbon emission, automotive industries are radically changing. Millions of electric vehicles are now on roads, consequently, tones of end-of-life batteries will come out after decades. In comparison to the number of end-of-life batteries and market size of battery recycling industries in the near future, the development of technology is still lag behind and the players in this field are not enough. Thus, our group set our sight on developing advanced battery recycling technology using hydrometallurgical processes. Battery recycling technology definitely will occupy highly demanding part in battery industries.
