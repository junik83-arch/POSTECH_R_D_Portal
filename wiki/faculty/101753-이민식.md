> 이민식 교수는 POSTECH에 소속되어 Tumor Microenvironment and Metabolism Lab(TME Lab)을 이끌고 있으며, 암세포의 변화된 대사 행동과 암이 자라는 미세환경(TME)의 대사적 특성을 규명하는 연구를 수행하고 있습니다. 연구실은 암 성장과 생존에 필수적인 영양소 대사를 찾아내고, 미세환경 내 암세포와 다양한 세포 종 사이의 대사적 경쟁·협력 및 면역억제적 대사 환경 형성 기전을 다루며, 궁극적으로 암세포를 '굶겨 죽이는' 새로운 개념의 항암 치료 개발을 목표로 합니다. 방법론으로는 멀티오믹스 분석과 함께 최신 유전자 조작 및 수술 동물(마우스) 모델, 암 환자의 생 검체를 활용해 핵심 대사 기전을 in vivo에서 검증하고 치료 표적으로서의 가능성을 평가합니다. 주요 연구 주제는 Tumor Microenvironment, Cancer Metabolism, Metastasis, T cell metabolism이며, 'Tumor Microenvironment and Metabolism' 과제를 2023년 10월부터 수행 중입니다. 대표 논문으로는 PI3K/AKT 활성화에 의한 PTEN 유비퀴틴화와 종양 형성 촉진(Nature Communications, 2015), 인슐린 수용체 기질 1·2 제거를 통한 Kras 유발 폐종양 억제(PNAS, 2018, 공동 제1저자), E3 유비퀴틴 리가아제 MKRN1 결손의 대사증후군 억제 효과(Nature Communications, 2018), Tip60 의존적 아세틸화를 통한 p21 안정화와 DNA 손상 반응(Cell Death & Differentiation, 2013) 등이 있습니다.

Tumor Microenvironment and Metabolism
Our lab conducts research that are contributing to the treatment of cancer by understanding the altered metabolic behaviors of cancer cells and identifying vital metabolic mechanisms for cancer growth and survival. The understanding of tumor metabolism has gained significant attention and has become the hallmark of cancer in recent decades. However, previous metabolic studies focused on cancer cells have had limitations due to the insufficient consideration of the tumor’s growing environment.
1. Our research team aims to analyze and elucidate the metabolic characteristics of the tumor microenvironment (TME) using cutting-edge technologies and ideas.
2. We observe how various types of cancer adapt to their unique metabolic environments through multi-omics approaches.
3. We further investigate key metabolic mechanisms required for tumor growth and survival in the context of in vivo using state-of-the-art genetically engineered mouse and surgical mouse models, and fresh tissue samples from cancer patients.
Our research encompasses the development of therapies, starting from fundamental research to assess whether these key metabolic mechanisms can be utilized as treatment targets.

In vivo Metabolic Vulnerability
Solid tumors grow and survive within a nutrient-restricted tumor microenvironment (TME) shaped by abnormal vascular architecture and fibrosis. Under these conditions, cancer cells activate diverse metabolic pathways to sustain proliferation and survival. However, most conventional metabolic studies have been conducted under nutrient-rich in vitro conditions, which inadequately capture the metabolic composition of the in vivo TME. As a result, many metabolism-targeted therapeutic strategies that showed promise in preclinical studies have repeatedly failed to demonstrate the expected efficacy in clinical trials.
To overcome these limitations, our laboratory aims to reconstruct metabolic conditions that more faithfully reflect the actual tumor environment by leveraging quantitative metabolomics data obtained directly from in vivo tumor tissues. Using this platform, we will systematically investigate how cancer cells rewire and adapt their intracellular metabolic networks under nutrient-limited conditions.
Through this approach, we seek to precisely characterize intracellular metabolic alterations of cancer cells in vivo, analyze the relationship between TME metabolite availability and intracellular metabolite states, and identify the metabolic pathways that become activated. Building on these findings, we will uncover metabolic dependencies that are not apparent under standard culture conditions but are essential for cancer cell survival and proliferation in vivo. Ultimately, this study aims to define in vivo metabolic vulnerabilities of solid tumors and propose new metabolism-based therapeutic targets that are grounded in the physiological tumor microenvironment.
고형암은 비정상적 혈관 구조와 섬유화로 영양소가 제한된 종양 미세환경(tumor microenvironment, TME)  내에서 성장하고 생존합니다. 이러한 환경에서 암세포는 증식과 생존을 유지하기 위해 다양한 대사 경로를 활성화하지만, 기존의 대사 연구 대부분은 영양소(Key nutrinet)가 풍부한 in vitro 조건에 기반하고 있어 실제 TME의 대사 조성을 충분히 반영하지 못하는 한계가 있습니다.

Overcoming dynamic environmental change during metastasis
Cancer metastasis is a fatal disease in which cancer cells originating from a primary tumor migrate to distant organs and successfully establish secondary lesions (colonization), ultimately leading to patient mortality. During the metastatic process, primary tumor cells transition into circulating tumor cells (CTCs), thereby detaching from the primary tumor microenvironment.
As a consequence, cancer cells are simultaneously exposed to profound extrinsic changes associated with the bloodstream a drastically different environment and intrinsic alterations resulting from the loss of cell–matrix adhesion (detachment).
Subsequently, disseminated cancer cells must adapt to the distinct microenvironment of distant organs and achieve successful colonization to survive and proliferate. Throughout this process, cancer cells encounter diverse stresses, including elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS). To overcome these stresses or sustain proliferation, cancer cells have to exploit metabolites available in their surrounding environment.
However, most studies on cancer metastasis to date have primarily focused on transcriptomic study, while investigations centered on metabolic changes throughout the metastatic cascade remain relatively limited.
Therefore, our laboratory aims to systematically elucidate the metabolic adaptations underlying cancer metastasis using metabolomic approaches, with particular emphasis on two critical hurdles: the formation of circulating tumor cells and the subsequent colonization of distant organs.
암 전이는 원발암에서 유래한 암세포가 원격 장기로 이동하여 정착(colonization)함으로써 궁극적으로 환자의 사망에 이르게 하는 치명적인 질환입니다. 원발암 세포는 전이 과정 중 circulating tumor cell (CTC) 로 전환되며, 이 과정에서 지속적으로 증식해오던 원발암 미세환경에서 이탈하게 됩니다. 그 결과, 암세포는 혈액이라는 전혀 다른 extrinsic 환경 변화와 동시에 세포–기질 접착 상실(detachment)에 따른 intrinsic 변화를 동시에 경험하게 됩니다. 이후 전이된 암세포는 원격 장기라는 새로운 미세환경에 적응하고, 생존 및 증식을 위해 성공적인 colonization을 수행해야 합니다. 이 과정에서 암세포는 활성산소종(reactive oxygen species, ROS)과 같은 다양한 스트레스에 노출되며, 이를 극복하고 증식을 유지하기 위해 필연적으로 주변 환경의 대사체를 활용할 수밖에 없습니다.
그러나 지금까지의 암 전이 연구는 주로 전사체(transcriptome) 수준의 변화에 초점을 맞추어 이루어져 왔으며, 전이 과정 전반에서의 대사적(metabolic) 변화를 중심으로 한 연구는 상대적으로 제한적이었습니다.
이에 본 연구실은 암 전이 과정에서 주요한 장벽(hurdle)으로 작용하는 circulating tumor cell 형성 단계와 원격 장기에서의 colonization 단계를 metabolomics 연구를 통해 체계적으로 규명하고자 합니다.
T cell metabolism
Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy, one of the immunotherapeutic approaches, has achieved unprecedented clinical success in hematological malignancies through antigen-specific recognition and potent cytotoxic activity. However, its efficacy in solid tumors remains limited. This limitation is attributed not only to antigen heterogeneity and physical barriers but also to the profoundly suppressive metabolic conditions of the tumor microenvironment (TME).
The tumor microenvironment is characterized by severe nutrient deprivation, hypoxia, acidosis, and the accumulation of immunosuppressive metabolites, all of which constrain the persistence and antitumor function of CAR-T cells. Following antigen recognition, CAR-T cells require rapid metabolic reprogramming to support robust proliferation and sustained effector functions. However, metabolic competition with tumor cells restricts access to essential nutrients, ultimately driving functional exhaustion of CAR-T cells.
Despite these challenges, most CAR-T–related studies to date have primarily focused on signaling pathways, gene expression, and the regulation of inhibitory immune receptors. In contrast, systematic analyses of the metabolic alterations experienced by CAR-T cells within the tumor microenvironment and their functional consequences remain relatively limited. Therefore, our laboratory aims to elucidate the key metabolic constraints encountered by CAR-T cells in the TME through metabolomics-based approaches and to develop metabolically enhanced next-generation CAR-T therapeutic strategies.

WHAT WE DO
How cancer cells alter their metabolism and shape their metabolic milieu?, how cancer and other types of cells harness nutrients in their local environments (or Tumor MicroEnvironment, TME) for their benefits?, how systemic metabolism of the host affects TME and in turn influences the metabolism of cancer cells are major areas of focus in our lab. We are working to identify aspects of nutrient metabolism that are required for tumor growth and survival, shaping immune-suppressive metabolic milieu and tumor-supporting crosstalk of cancer cells and other cell types in TME. Ultimately, we aim to harness our knowledges of nutrient metabolism to combat cancer.
