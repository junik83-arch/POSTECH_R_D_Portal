> 김지훈 교수는 POSTECH 물리학과 소속으로 Magnetic Force Microscopy(MFM) 연구실을 이끌며, 자기력현미경을 이용해 초전도와 자성을 연구하고 있습니다. 연구실은 저온·고안정 자기력현미경 장비 개발을 핵심 축으로 삼아 압저항 캔틸레버를 장착한 극저온 이중 스캐너 MFM, 자기장 방향을 3차원으로 제어하는 벡터필드 극저온 MFM, 그리고 서브켈빈 MFM 플랫폼을 직접 구축했습니다. 이를 통해 중페르미온 초전도체 CeCoIn5의 절대 런던 침투깊이와 개별 아브리코소프 소용돌이의 피닝력 측정, 기하학적으로 구속된 초전도 박막에서의 1차원 비양자화 자속 및 소용돌이 조작 연구를 수행했습니다. 자성 분야에서는 반데르발스 자성체 CrGeTe3, Fe4GeTe2, Fe3GaTe2의 국소 자기 도메인 구조와 자기장·온도에 따른 변화를 벡터필드 MFM으로 규명했습니다. 또한 Nb/CeRu2Ga2B 같은 강자성-초전도 하이브리드에서 소용돌이에 의한 자기 도메인 제어, 고엔트로피 초전도 합금, 무자기장 메모리 프로그래머블 초전도 다이오드 등 초전도-자성 결합 소자 연구도 함께 진행하고 있습니다.

Instrumentation
■ Cryogenic magnetic force microscopy platforms
Instrumentation development is a central part of the MFM Laboratory. We design and construct cryogenic magnetic force microscopy platforms that enable quantitative imaging of superconducting vortices, magnetic domains, and hybrid quantum materials under controlled temperature and magnetic-field conditions.
Our MFM systems combine low-temperature operation, high mechanical stability, sensitive force detection, and vector-field control. These capabilities allow the laboratory to go beyond conventional magnetic imaging and perform local, quantitative measurements of penetration depth, vortex pinning, vortex manipulation, and magnetic-domain evolution.
■ Cryogenic dual-scanner MFM with piezoresistive cantilever
We developed a cryogenic dual-scanner magnetic force microscope equipped with a piezoresistive cantilever. The piezoresistive detection scheme removes the need for optical alignment during temperature changes and provides a stable platform for low-temperature MFM measurements.
The dual-scanner design combines coarse and fine scanning capabilities, enabling flexible sample positioning and high-resolution local imaging. This system is particularly useful for comparative studies of superconducting vortices and magnetic textures over a wide temperature range.
Jungsub Lee and Jeehoon Kim,
“Construction of a cryogenic dual scanner magnetic force microscope equipped with piezoresistive cantilever,” Review of Scientific Instruments

■ Vector-field cryogenic MFM
We constructed a vector-field cryogenic magnetic force microscope to investigate magnetic and superconducting phenomena under independently controlled magnetic-field directions. Vector-field capability is essential for studying anisotropic superconductors, chiral magnetic textures, vortex configurations, and magnetic-domain evolution under tilted magnetic fields.
This platform enables three-dimensional control of the magnetic field while maintaining cryogenic MFM imaging capability, providing a powerful experimental tool for spatially resolved studies of superconductors, van der Waals magnets, and topological magnetic materials.
Geunyong Kim, Jinyoung Yun, Yeonkyu Lee, and Jeehoon Kim,
“Construction of a vector-field cryogenic magnetic force microscope,” Review of Scientific Instruments

■ Sub-Kelvin MFM for heavy-fermion superconductors
Sub-Kelvin MFM provides a local probe of superconductivity in strongly correlated materials. Using this technique, we characterized the heavy-fermion superconductor CeCoIn
5
by measuring the absolute London penetration depth and its temperature dependence at ultra-low temperatures.
The same approach enables direct measurement of the pinning force of individual Abrikosov vortices and local evaluation of superconducting inhomogeneity on the micrometer scale. This demonstrates the strength of cryogenic MFM as a quantitative local probe for unconventional superconductors.
Dirk Wulferding, Geunyong Kim, Hoon Kim, Ilkyu Yang, E. D. Bauer, F. Ronning, R. Movshovich, and Jeehoon Kim,

■ Superconducting vortices and penetration depth by MFM
We use cryogenic magnetic force microscopy (MFM) to image individual Abrikosov vortices and quantify local magnetic responses in superconductors. Comparative MFM measurements allow us to extract the penetration depth and vortex pinning forces with spatial resolution, enabling direct studies of inhomogeneous superconducting states.
This research provides microscopic information on vortex configurations, local superconducting properties, and magnetic-field penetration, which are essential for understanding unconventional, mesoscopic, and spatially inhomogeneous superconductors.

■ One-dimensional vortex interaction and unquantized magnetic flux
Geometrically confined superconducting films can host vortex-antivortex structures connected by one-dimensional unquantized magnetic flux. Vector-field MFM enables controlled creation, manipulation, and imaging of these one-dimensional interacting vortices.
By directly visualizing vortex motion and vortex-antivortex interactions, we explore new routes toward braiding-related physics and topological superconducting systems.

■ Interplay between metastable magnetic domains and superconducting vortices
We investigate the interplay between superconductivity and magnetism in ferromagnet–superconductor hybrid structures. In a Nb/CeRu

■ Giant negative spin polarization in superconducting high-entropy alloy NbTaTiZrFe
We study superconducting high-entropy alloy systems in which superconductivity, magnetism, and strong electronic correlations coexist. In NbTaTiZrFe, strong Coulomb interaction induces giant negative spin polarization, revealing an unusual coupling between superconducting and magnetic responses.
This work highlights high-entropy superconductors as a promising material platform for exploring unconventional spin-polarized superconducting states and correlation-driven magnetic phenomena.

■ Field-free memory-programmable superconducting diode
We develop superconducting diode devices based on simple magnetic/superconducting thin-film structures. In these devices, magnetic memory and superconducting vortex dynamics produce nonreciprocal superconducting transport without requiring an external magnetic field.
The field-free memory-programmable superconducting diode demonstrates robust superconducting rectification with high diode efficiency. This approach provides a route toward superconducting electronics, cryogenic memory elements, and future quantum-device architectures.
