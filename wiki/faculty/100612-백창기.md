> 백창기 교수는 POSTECH IT융합공학과 소속으로 Innovative Device Engineering and Application(IDEA) Lab.을 이끌고 있으며, 로직·메모리, 뉴로모픽 컴퓨팅, 열전 기술 분야의 첨단 나노소자 연구를 수행하고 있습니다. 연구실은 3D 양자 시뮬레이션과 나노공정을 기반으로 기초 발견에서 상용화 가능한 기술 구현까지를 목표로 하며, FinFET, 커패시터 없는(capacitor-less) 메모리, 실리콘 나노구조 광소자, 인공 뉴런 회로 등을 다룹니다. 최근에는 실리콘 나노튜브에서 포논 국소화(phonon localization) 현상을 규명해 열전도도를 낮추는 연구를 Nano Energy에 발표했으며, 이는 폐열을 전기로 바꾸는 차세대 열전소자 기술로 다수 언론에 보도되었습니다. 또한 나노튜브 어레이 기반 열전소자, 커패시터리스 메모리 소자 등에 대한 국내외 특허를 다수 출원·등록했습니다. 수상 실적으로는 2023년 삼성전자 DS부문 산학협력 교류회 우수특허상 최우수상, 2017년 과학기술정보통신부 장관상 수상이 있습니다.

Logic & Memory Technology
A Fin Field-effect transistor (FinFET) is a MOSFET built on a substrate where the gate is placed on two, three, or four sides of the channel or wrapped around the channel, forming a double gate structure. These devices have been given the generic name “finfets” because the source/drain region forms fins on the silicon surface. The FinFET devices have significantly faster switching times and higher current density than the mainstream CMOS technology.
Dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) is a type of random access semiconductor memory that stores each bit of data in a separate tiny capacitor within an integrated circuit. The capacitor can either be charged or discharged; these two states are taken to represent the two values of a bit, conventionally called 0 and 1. The electric charge on the capacitors slowly leaks off, so without intervention the data on the chip would soon be lost. To prevent this, DRAM requires an external memory refresh circuit which periodically rewrites the data in the capacitors, restoring them to their original charge. This refresh process is the defining characteristic of dynamic random-access memory, in contrast to static random-access memory (SRAM) which does not require data to be refreshed. Unlike flash memory, DRAM is volatile memory (vs. non-volatile memory), since it loses its data quickly when power is removed. However, DRAM does exhibit limited data remanence.
Neuromorphic Computing Technology
Neuromorphic computing is a form of computing inspired by the structure and function of the human brain. Using artificial neurons and synapses, neuromorphic computers simulate the way our brains process information. This allows them to solve problems, recognize patterns, and make decisions more quickly and energy-efficiently than the Von-Neumann computers we commonly use today.
Thermoelectric Technology
A thermoelectric device creates voltage when there is a different temperature on each side. Conversely, when a voltage is applied to it, it creates a temperature difference. At the atomic scale, an applied temperature gradient causes charge carriers in the material to diffuse from the hot side to the cold side. Thermoelectric effect can be used to generate electricity, measure temperature or change the temperature of objects. Because the direction of heating and cooling is determined by the polarity of the applied voltage, thermoelectric devices can be used as temperature controllers.
