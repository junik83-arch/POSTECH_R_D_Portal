> 용기중 교수는 POSTECH 화학공학과 소속으로 전자재료표면화학연구실(SCHEMA, Surface Chemistry Laboratory of Energy/Electronic Materials)을 이끌고 있으며, 표면화학과 전기화학 기법을 바탕으로 차세대 에너지·전자 재료를 연구합니다. 에너지 소재 분야에서는 전이금속 수산화물·인화물·황화물 등 나노소재 촉매를 이용한 물 분해 수소 생산, 요소·하이드라진 산화를 활용한 하이브리드 수전해, 음이온교환막 기반 대면적 MEA 수전해 시스템, 태양전지와 결합한 자가구동형 수전해를 다루고 있습니다. 또한 상온·상압에서 암모니아를 합성해 하버-보슈 공정을 대체할 수 있는 리튬 매개 질소환원반응(Li-NRR)과 전기화학적·광촉매적 질산염 환원 연구를 수행하며, 관련 성과가 ACS Energy Letters, Advanced Energy Materials, Advanced Science, Chemical Engineering Journal 등에 발표되었습니다. 전자 공정 분야에서는 액체-고체 마찰전기 나노발전기(L-S TENG) 기반 자가구동 센서와 원자층증착(ALD) 공정 및 산소확산 차단층 연구를 진행하고 있습니다. 홈페이지에는 그린 암모니아 생산 효율 향상, 가시광선과 적외선 영역에서 모두 작동하는 광촉매 개발 등 연구팀 성과 소식이 소개되어 있으며, 연구참여생·대학원생·박사후연구원을 모집 중입니다.

Overall water splitting with nanomaterials catalyst
Water splitting is a broad term referring to a chemical process where water is separated into oxygen and hydrogen. Efficient and cost-effective water splitting is crucial for the development of a hydrogen economy. Electrochemical water splitting, in particular, is recognized as the most promising technology for green hydrogen production. We are synthesizing nanomaterial electrode catalysts suitable for use in water electrolysis systems, employing various transition metals. Different synthesis methods, such as hydrothermal, thermal evaporation, chemical vapor deposition (CVD), and electrodeposition, are utilized to create transition metal compounds with diverse compositions, including hydroxides, phosphides, and sulfides.
Furthermore, we have successfully integrated photovoltaic (PV) cells to establish self-powered electrochemical systems. A series-connected PV cell provides sufficient electrical power to drive electrochemical reactions. We have also developed a self-powered water electrolysis system that operates solely on solar energy by coupling the PV cell with the electrolysis cell, allowing for solar-driven water splitting.

Hybrid water splitting
Overall water splitting involves the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) for hydrogen production and the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) for oxygen generation. Given its 4-electron process, the OER exhibits slow reactivity and demands relatively high overpotentials. To address these issues, we explore hybrid water splitting that substitute the OER with reactions like urea oxidation or hydrazine oxidation, which have lower theoretical potentials. These alternatives aim to enhance the efficiency of water splitting by addressing the sluggish kinetics and high overpotential associated with the OER.

Membrane electrode assembly (MEA)
Conventional studies on water electrolysis have been conducted in batch systems with a lab-scale area ranging from 0.25 to 0.5 cm². However, the commercialization of green hydrogen production through water electrolysis requires the development of large-area cell systems utilizing Membrane Electrode Assemblies (MEA). MEA typically involves combining a large-area catalyst with proton exchange membranes, anion exchange membranes, bipolar membranes, and so on, to form the system. Our research specifically focuses on MEA systems that utilize anion exchange membranes, allowing for the utilization of non-precious metal catalysts. We are actively involved in exploring and advancing the application of non-precious metal catalysts in large-scale water electrolysis for efficient green hydrogen production.
Reference paper:
cej.2024.157106
Electrochemical Lithium-mediated Nitrogen Reduction Reaction (Li-NRR)
The Li-mediated Nitrogen Reduction Reaction (Li-NRR) literally refers to a process in which lithium mediates the reduction of nitrogen, ultimately aiming to synthesize ammonia. Ammonia, a key component in the production of fertilizers and various chemical industries, is the second most synthesized chemical in the world. Currently, the primary method for ammonia synthesis, the Haber-Bosch process, requires high temperatures of 350–450°C and high pressures of 150–200 atmospheres, resulting in approximately 1% of the world's energy being consumed solely for ammonia production. In contrast, Li-NRR can synthesize ammonia under ambient temperature and pressure, positioning it as a next-generation environmentally friendly green ammonia synthesis technology that could replace the Haber-Bosch process. We are conducting various studies to enhance the selectivity and conversion efficiency of Li-NRR.
Li-NRR fundamentally reduces nitrogen under non-aqueous conditions, and by altering various parameters such as the type of lithium salt, solvent, electrolyte additives, and electrodes, the selectivity and conversion efficiency of Li-NRR can be improved. These studies help identify factors that influence Li-NRR's selectivity and conversion efficiency, as well as uncover its mechanisms. For example, we have used dimethyl sulfide, a sulfur-containing compound, as an electrolyte additive to design the physicochemical structure of the solid-electrolyte interface (SEI), thereby achieving a steady cell potential. Through electrolyte modification, we have developed localized-high concentration electrolytes and steric hindrance-driven electrolytes, which form SEIs that are favorable for Li-NRR. These efforts have consistently enhanced the selectivity of Li-NRR and contributed significantly to the advancement of the field.

Electrochemical Nitrate Reduction Reaction (NORR)
The electrochemical nitrate reduction reaction (NORR) enables the sustainable synthesis of green ammonia by electrochemically reducing nitrate (NO₃⁻). Ammonia is the second most-produced chemical globally, primarily serving as a key precursor in fertilizers and chemical manufacturing. More recently, it has garnered attention as a hydrogen carrier due to its high hydrogen content and energy density. However, the conventional Haber-Bosch process operates under extreme temperature and pressure conditions, resulting in substantial CO₂ emissions and raising significant environmental concerns. To address these challenges, NORR has emerged as a promising alternative, utilizing nitrate from groundwater to contribute to pollution reduction while achieving high ammonia yield and excellent Faradaic efficiency. Compared to the nitrogen reduction reaction (NRR), NORR exhibits superior selectivity due to the favorable reduction properties of nitrogen oxides, establishing it as a viable pathway for renewable ammonia production.
We primarily focus on developing catalysts for NORR systems, synthesizing high-performance nanostructured catalysts using a variety of metals. In addition to utilizing equipment such as sputtering systems and furnaces, we employ diverse fabrication techniques, including electrodeposition, hydrothermal synthesis, and photodeposition, to optimize electrochemical catalyst performance. Furthermore, we investigate energy-efficient applications, including anodic coupling with alternative reactions and the assembly of Zn-nitrate batteries. From a systems perspective, we also strive to enhance the practical scalability of NORR by developing flow-based systems for large-scale ammonia production.
Solar Hydrogen Production
Solar hydrogen production has been regarded as a key technology for solving the environmental issues because it utilizes two earth-abundant and clean sources, light and water. However, photocatalytic hydrogen production has a relatively low solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency, due to the inefficient utilization of sunlight, charge recombination and sluggish kinetics of the surface reactions. Therefore, designing photocatalyst with broadened light absorption, enhanced charge carrier mobility and fastened reaction kinetics for hydrogen production is crucial in this field. To date, our group has investigated various particulate photocatalysts and photoelectrodes for solar hydrogen production.
Particulate Photocatalyst
Particulate photocatalyst is one of the simplest and cheapest method among the solar hydrogen production methods. However, the particle itself should have sufficient capability without external assistance. To enhance the efficiency of photocatalysts, we explored various catalytic engineering strategies such as cocatalyst deposition and heterojunction. Utilizing cocatalysts are one of the effective methods to facilitate the reaction kinetics because it can reduce activation energy of the process and serve as electron sink for enhanced charge separation. Typically, noble metals like Pt, Ru, and Au were employed. Furthermore, our group actively investigated heterojunction photocatalyst. The construction of heterostructure starts from attaching two types of semiconductor with unequal band structure. Our group focused on fabricating Z-scheme and type II heterojunction, which enabled efficient charge separation and enhanced light absorption, synergistically enhancing the overall solar-to-hydrogen efficiency.
Reference: Adv. Mater. 2023, 35, 2200172
Photoelectrochemical Cell (PEC)
In a PEC system, photoelectrodes are connected to charge collectors, providing extra potential needed for hydrogen production in addition to external power. Water is broken down into hydrogen and oxygen by electrolysis, but the electrical energy is obtained by a photoelectrochemical cell (PEC) process. The system is also named artificial photosynthesis. We have developed many types of PEC cells which consists of photoanode or photocathode and counter electrode. We developed metal oxides like ZnO, TiO2, WO3 as scaffold materials for photoanode, while BiVO4, CdS, CdSe are used to modify the surface of metal oxide scaffold. We studied charge transfer mechanism, doping analysis and co-catalysts to improve performance of the photoanode as well. Also, we investigated CIGS and p-Si for photocathode with non-noble metal co-catalyst, Ni-Mo.
Reference: Adv. Mater. 2017, 29, 1702431
Photocatalytic Nitrate (NO3-) Reduction to Ammonia (NH3) (PcNRA)
Ammonia (NH3) is indispensable chemical for its versatility in industrial and agricultural fields. Its usage has recently been extended as a carbon-free hydrogen carrier with its high hydrogen density. However, the conventional Haber-Bosch process has been criticized due to considerable amount of energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions. Among the alternatives of environment-friendly ammonia production methods, photocatalytic nitrate (NO3-)-to-ammonia (PcNRA) is considered as an efficient strategy for green ammonia production. Moreover, NO3- is considered as organic pollutant in wastewater. Since PcNRA converts organic waste into value-added product, this can be a promising approach considering environmental concerns. There are two major pathways in photocatalytic nitrate reduction, nitrogen (N2) production and NH3 production. NO3—to-N2 pathway involves five electrons, while NO3--to-NH3 involves eight electrons. Additionally, the hydrogen evolution reaction involves only two electrons. Consequently, PcNRA has two kinetically favorable side reactions, which makes the primary purpose of this research to suppress the production of side products and enhance the NH3 selectivity. Our group has investigated efficient photocatalysts for PcNRA. We develop metal oxide-based catalysts like TiO2, SrTiO3 and organic semiconductors like g-C3N4. Our research specifically focuses on various catalytic engineering strategies like heteroatom doping, metal deposition and single atom catalysts. We actively work on developing photocatalysts for efficient and selective PcNRA.
Reference: Sol. RRL 2024, 8, 2300880

Liquid-Solid triboelectric nanogenerator (L-S TENG)
Triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs) convert mechanical energy into electrical energy through triboelectrification and electrostatic induction, providing a renewable power source. While solid–solid TENGs are valued for their easy fabrication and low cost, liquid–solid (L-S) TENGs, especially water droplet-driven TENGs, offer advantages by harvesting energy from rain, rivers, and ocean waves. Expanding TENG applications requires intelligent low-power strategies, such as reversible switching of electrical output in response to external stimuli, enabling self-powered sensors and switches.
Our group has developed self-powered sensor systems based on L-S TENGs. We aim to optimize TENG performance by utilizing surface structure modifications and material property transitions in response to external stimuli, advancing them into sensor and energy conversion devices. Through this research, we seek practical applications in various fields, including smart sensors, wearable devices, and eco-friendly energy solutions.

Atomic layer deposition
Atomic Layer Chemical Vapor Deposition(ALCVD) is vapor deposition method, which precursor and reactant are injected in the reactor alternatively. Different from normal MOCVD process, it is self-limited surface reaction which is affected by the surface temperature. Monolayer of material is deposited by repeated exposure of precursor and reactant. ALCVD process is a key process in the fabrication of semiconductor device because of these deposition characteristics.
Oxygen diffusion blocking layer (ODBL)
In atomic layer deposition (ALD) of high-k materials, an oxygen diffusion blocking layer is essential to prevent unwanted oxygen penetration, which can degrade interface quality and electrical performance. This layer acts as a barrier, maintaining the desired stoichiometry of the high-k film and preventing oxidation-induced defects in the underlying substrate. Effective blocking layers, such as metal nitrides or oxides, enhance device reliability and electrical stability, making them crucial for high-performance electronic applications.
