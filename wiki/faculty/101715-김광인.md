Research
Vision-based robot interaction
Training robots to perform manipulation tasks from visual inputs is a challenging task. A common approach is to train an end-to-end model from scratch using domain-specific data of robots interacting with the physical world. However, collecting such large and diverse datasets can be expensive and may lack generalizability. We are exploring diverse research avenues in this domain, such as extracting of generic visual representations from human video data and incorporating natural language cues to guide robot manipulation. This could circumvent the need for labor-intensive data collection specific to each robotic task, enabling robots to excel across a spectrum of unseen scenarios.
Tabular data understanding
While research based on unstructured data (such as images and text) has seen extensive. During its development, there has been a shortage of studies focused on extracting information from structured data, particularly tables. Large language models (LLMs) struggle with understanding two-dimensional structures like tables, as they are primarily trained on one-dimensional text data. To address this limitation, we are conducting research on table question answering (QA), aiming to enhance LLM’s understanding of tables through techniques such as fine-tuning or prompt engineering.
Retrieval augmented generation
While large language models (LLMs) exhibit impressive performance, they face challenges in encapsulating all information within their parameters and updating to incorporate the latest knowledge. In response, retrieval augmented generation (RAG) emerges as a promising solution. We are investigating question answering (QA) utilizing RAG. Our focus lies in employing various modalities such as tables, text, images, etc. We are particularly delving into multi-hop QA, where answering questions require gathering information from multiple pieces of evidence or sources.
Black box optimization
We address the challenge of optimizing functions in the absence of derivative information. While zeroth-order optimization shows promise in solving such problems, its applications have been restricted to low-dimensional scenarios. Our goal is to overcome these limitations, unlocking the potential for black box optimization in contexts such as LLM adaptation and privacy-preserving distributed learning.

Open positions
[
PhD and Masters in Machine Learning or Computer Vision]
We are seeking highly motivated candidates to apply for our PhD and Masters programs in Machine Learning or Computer Vision. These programs are offered by either the
Department of Electronic Engineering
or the
Graduate School of Artificial Intelligence
at POSTECH.
If you are considering joining our group, kindly assess if you meet the following four requirements:
Mathematical maturity:
Mathematics is the language of AI. You should have a strong background in linear algebra, probability theory & statistics, and vector calculus at the undergraduate engineering level (e.g., EE and CSE). If you work on more theoretically oriented problems, you should be ready to learn more advanced techniques and tools such as optimization, and real and functional analysis.
Programming skills:
You should have experience in programming, particularly in Python, which is one of the main programming languages in machine learning. Knowledge of other programming languages and environments, such as C and C++, is also useful. As a beginning graduate student, you don’t have to be an expert in programming, but you should be able to acquire skills quickly.
Communication skills:
Conducting research is about discovering new insights and effectively communicating them to others. Therefore, it is essential that you possess the ability to read and analyze academic work, formally present your findings and ideas, and engage in discussions with your fellow students and supervisors. Additionally, since a significant portion of scientific work is conducted in English, proficiency in English is necessary. While you do not have to be an expert in English at the beginning of your graduate studies, you should strive to improve your proficiency in the language over time.
Background in machine learning and/or computer vision:
You should have a fundamental understanding of machine learning or computer vision at the undergraduate level. Having conducted undergraduate research or development projects in these fields is beneficial, but it is not mandatory.
If you possess the above-mentioned skills and background and are interested in joining our team, please email Dr. Kwang In Kim at [이메일] and attach a copy of your undergraduate academic transcripts (including rank). Please include either `[PhD]’ or `[Masters]’ in the subject line of your email, depending on the program you are applying for.
