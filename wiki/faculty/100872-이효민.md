> 이효민 교수는 POSTECH 화학공학과 부교수로, 연성 물질 및 기능성 계면 연구실(SMFIL, Soft Matter and Functional Interfaces Laboratory)을 이끌고 있습니다. 서울대학교에서 화학생물공학 학사(2009)를, MIT에서 화학공학 박사(2014)를 받은 뒤 하버드대학교에서 박사후연구원으로 일했고, 2017년 POSTECH 조교수로 부임해 2021년부터 부교수로 재직 중입니다. 연구실은 나노에서 매크로 스케일에 이르는 계면에서의 연성 물질 구조와 거동을 이해·제어해 기능성 소재를 개발하며, 젖음성 제어, 하이드로젤, 마이크로패터닝, 미세유체, 인공세포, 암호화·디스플레이, 헬스케어·지속가능성 등을 주요 주제로 삼고 있습니다. 대표 성과로는 김서림 방지 표면과 초소수성 코팅, 야누스 입자 및 피커링 에멀전, 자극 응답형 하이드로젤·왁스 마이크로캡슐 관련 표지 논문과 다수의 국내 등록특허가 있습니다. 수상 경력으로는 한국고분자학회 신진학술상(2020), 한국화학공학회 전달현상 부문 신진과학자상(2024), 차세대과학기술한림원(Y-KAST) 회원 선출(2026) 등이 있습니다.

Use soft things to solve hard problems
The overall aim of our research is to understand and control the structure and dynamics of soft matter at interfaces in dimensions ranging from nano- to macroscale and engineer new functional materials. A key aspect of our research is the preparation of well-defined molecules, materials, emulsions and surfaces, which leads to desired function.

Wetting
The ability to control surface wettability plays an important role in many applications, from antifogging and self-cleaning surfaces to enhancing condensation. By manipulating surface energy and microstructural design, we aim to develop hydrophilic and hydrophobic surfaces tailored for specific functionalities. These designs enable the effective management of water droplets, leading to advancements in antifogging coatings, liquid-repellent surfaces, and more.
Hydrogel
Hydrogels, known for their high water content and tunable mechanical properties, serve as versatile materials in designing functional interfaces. We explore their applications in creating antifogging layers, responsive coatings, and soft actuators. By adjusting chemical compositions and crosslinking densities, we tailor hydrogels to exhibit specific responses to environmental stimuli such as humidity and temperature, broadening their utility across diverse fields.
Micropatterning
Micropatterning techniques enable precise spatial control over material properties and surface functionalities. Using advanced methods like lithography, we fabricate microstructured surfaces that enhance optical transparency, mechanical stability, and environmental resilience. These patterned surfaces find applications in flexible electronics, optical devices, and multifunctional coatings that integrate hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions seamlessly.
Nanofibers
Electrospinning is a versatile technique for generating continuous nanofibers with high surface area, enhancing the wettability of materials. These nanofiber matrices exhibit a high surface area, interconnected pores, and thin permeation paths. By exploiting these properties, we aim to develop high-performance membranes with superior permeability for various applications.
Multi-phase Emulsion
Multi-phase emulsions, with their intricate structures, offer a platform where composition and structure can be precisely regulated through advanced microfluidic techniques. These systems enable independent control of inner and outer droplets, making them highly suitable for applications in sophisticated material encapsulation and controlled release. By leveraging diverse interfacial-active materials such as particles and surfactants, we fine-tune the physical and chemical properties of the emulsion shell, enabling selective material release and precise control over phase transitions. These emulsions are particularly promising as drug delivery systems and as microenvironments for catalytic reactions.
Vesicles and Microcapsules
Droplet microfluidics offers precise and simultaneous control over flow conditions, enabling the production of multiple emulsion droplets with tunable composition and structure. These droplets serve as versatile templates for incorporating unique functionalities tailored to specific applications. For instance, we utilize temperature-responsive materials in Janus particles to enhance emulsion stability while enabling on-demand coalescence. Additionally, we design shell membranes for double emulsion droplets and microcapsules to regulate material transport and utilize them in advanced smart release systems.
3D Printing
3D printing presents a groundbreaking approach to fabricating microfluidic devices, enabling the construction of microchannels with complex geometries. This technique allows for controlled outputs on the scale of tens of micrometers, facilitating the creation of fully three-dimensional channels and intricate surface modifications. Compared to traditional microfluidic fabrication methods, 3D printing enhances parallelization, screening capabilities, and mass production efficiency. It is widely applicable to various liquid-based processes, including micromixers, solution exchange, filtration, and droplet microfluidics.
Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)
Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is an indispensable tool for numerically analyzing and predicting fluid behavior, enabling the study of complex flow phenomena as well as thermal and mass transport across multiple scales. Our research leverages high-performance computing and diverse numerical methods to address challenges in fluid mechanics and uncover transformative insights. In real-world systems, multifaceted flow regimes—spanning laminar, turbulent, single-phase, and multiphase flows—frequently coexist. Through CFD simulations, we replicate these intricate dynamics with high fidelity, providing insights that are difficult to achieve experimentally. This approach improves predictive model reliability for design parameters such as flow distribution, pressure drops, temperature gradients, and concentration profiles. CFD also facilitates the transition from laboratory or pilot-scale verifications to full-scale commercial implementations by identifying and addressing latent flow issues, optimizing energy utilization, reaction efficiency, and production throughput. By integrating CFD, we aim to pioneer efficient and visionary fluid engineering solutions and foster cross-disciplinary innovation.
Encryption and Display
Optical encryption technologies and dynamic displays are revolutionized through the integration of wettability patterning and structural color. By leveraging multi-level wettability systems and programmable photoreactive polymers, we create advanced anti-counterfeiting measures and transformational full-color devices. These innovations offer unprecedented control over color and information display, paving the way for secure, interactive, and visually striking solutions in information security and consumer electronics.
Artificial Cell
Cells perform a variety of complex biochemical reactions orchestrated in space and time. These features have driven advancements in biotechnology, biosensors, biogenesis, bioreactors, and drug delivery. To replicate and understand these cellular functions, we develop artificial cell-like vesicles that mimic key cellular hallmarks, including metabolic pathways, material transport, and protein synthesis. Using droplet microfluidics, we produce monodisperse vesicles with highly efficient encapsulation and compartmentalization of biomolecules. These artificial cells provide a new avenue for designing responsive and programmable protocell systems through a bottom-up approach.
Healthcare and Sustainability
Conventional polymer materials are typically incinerated or landfilled, causing significant environmental and ecological harm. To address this, biodegradable materials have garnered considerable attention due to their ability to decompose into water and carbon dioxide through microbial activity. This environmentally friendly process eliminates the need for external energy inputs. Our research focuses on designing functional interfaces using these biodegradable materials, ensuring sustainability and contributing to environmental preservation.
