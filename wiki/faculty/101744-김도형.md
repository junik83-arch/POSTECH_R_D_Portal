> 김도형 교수는 POSTECH에 소속되어 신경 제어 연구실(Laboratory of Neural Control)을 이끌고 있으며, 생쥐 행동, 뇌졸중, 뇌-기계 인터페이스(BMI)를 주요 연구 주제로 삼고 있습니다. 연구실은 뇌가 유연한 행동을 학습하고 계산해 내는 고차 인지 기능의 신경 메커니즘을 규명하고, 이를 바탕으로 효율적인 뇌-기계 인터페이스 통신 알고리즘을 개발하는 데 초점을 둡니다. 이를 위해 Neuropixels 신경 탐침, 칼슘 이미징, 설치류 가상현실 시스템, 광유전학, 초고속 BMI 등 최신 기술을 활용하며, 기존의 신경 표상 해독 방식의 한계를 넘어 표상을 신경 회로에 통합하는 접근을 지향합니다. 대표 연구로는 작업기억에서 파브알부민 및 소마토스타틴 발현 억제성 뉴런의 서로 다른 역할을 규명한 Neuron(2016) 논문이 있고, 고밀도 전극에서 단일 뉴런 해상도의 저지연 스파이크 분류를 다룬 연구(bioRxiv, 2023)에도 참여했습니다. 또한 급성 허혈성 뇌졸중의 이차 예방, 항혈소판 치료 비교 등 대규모 임상·역학 연구에도 제1저자 또는 공동저자로 다수 참여한 이력이 있습니다.

Research
The brain has evolved to control a complex biological device: the body. It is so flexible and adaptive that our brain can learn to control other devices and communicate with other agents through our body. By leveraging this remarkable nature of the brain, brain-machine interface (BMI) tries to provide a direct link between a brain and a machine for control and communication. BMI has great potential for the restoration of sensory and motor function to help patients with peripheral neuropathy, spinal cord injury, stroke, or amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.
Recent advances in neuroscience, machine learning, and technology have contributed to improvements in the speed and accuracy of brain-machine interface (BMI) communication. However, several limitations still exist for its practical use.
1. Neural recording quality: While there have been improvements, the stability of neural recordings can still be a challenge, often requiring frequent recalibration of the decoder. This is because neural signals can vary over time due to factors such as electrode drift or changes in brain activity patterns.
2. Dynamic nature of neural representations: Research has shown that neural representations for sensory, motor, and abstract information can change over time. This raises questions about the feasibility of traditional decoding-based approaches, which rely on stable neural representations. Adapting BMI systems to account for these dynamic changes is an active area of research.
3. Decoding novel or unexperienced objects: The brain lacks neural representations for previously unexperienced or novel objects, making it impossible to decode these representations. While it is true that decoding novel objects can be challenging, recent research has explored methods such as transfer learning or generative models to address this limitation and improve decoding accuracy.
4. For individuals who have experienced stroke and lost function in their motor cortex, it can be challenging to find motor representations in other areas of the brain. We need to train alternative brain regions to control motor functions.
5. Limited channel count: Most commonly used neural probes still have a relatively low channel count, which can limit the amount of information that can be extracted from the brain. However, advancements in electrode technology are being pursued to increase channel counts and enable more comprehensive neural recordings.
Therefore, we need to step back and take a different approach, rather than finding the neural representation, but instead integrate these representations into the neural circuit. Our lab aims to build an efficient protocol to communicate with the brain for practical BMI.
